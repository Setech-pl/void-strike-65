## Findings

### M5-01 — hit sound reads POT2 instead of its previous frequency

**Location:** `src/main.s:7176-7186`, `:7217-7226`; compare the intended
phase reconstruction at `:3016-3025`.

`play_hit_sound` writes `$20` to AUDF2, `$88` to AUDC2 and 14 to `hit_timer`.
Each subsequent `update_sound` decrements the timer and executes:

```asm
inc AUDF2
inc AUDF2
```

POKEY does not read back AUDF2. Reading `$D202` reads POT2; the two increments
therefore do not add two to the previously written sound divider. With a
stable paddle reading they both write the same paddle-derived value plus one.
This is an active sound path in the shipped image, not an unused debug routine.
The pause-resume code explicitly reconstructs the intended divider as
`$3C - 2 * hit_timer`, independently confirming the intended progression.

**Evidence and reproduction.** I loaded the current linked ATR runtime with
`scripts/weapon-pickup-runtime.mjs`, called `play_hit_sound`, then called
`update_sound` four times on `Nmos6502`. The control used the harness's ordinary
RAM-backed I/O. The hardware-aware cases supplied a read hook for `$D202` and
recorded the writes. No executable byte was changed.

| `$D202` read model | Divider initially written | Divider after updates 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Ordinary RAM, as in the CPU harness | 32 | 34, 36, 38, 40 |
| POT2 fixed at 0 | 32 | 1, 1, 1, 1 |
| POT2 fixed at 64 | 32 | 65, 65, 65, 65 |
| POT2 fixed at 228 | 32 | 229, 229, 229, 229 |

The hardware model is independently supported by Atari800 7.1.2's
`src/pokey.c:104-119` (`POKEY_GetByte`: addresses below 8 read
`POKEY_POT_input`, capped by the paddle scan). That source was obtained at
commit `57d24b1f14b3b7b3b0ed3fdcc694db0fba7f8b46` in
`build/atari800-trace`. This is a register-level result, not a claimed acoustic
measurement or a claim that every paddle/scan state equals 228.

**Why the existing process missed it.** The adjacent player-fire path already
avoids this exact hardware mistake by keeping its phase in RAM. Its regression
protection is specific to AUDF4. Music tests check channel-2 preemption and
control-byte ownership, while ordinary RAM-backed reads make AUDF2 appear to
support read-modify-write. Passing those tests cannot validate this sweep.
This defect predates the audited diff and was found in the rotating sound review.

**Recommended correction and regression:** derive the divider from the hit's
RAM phase/timer and write it. Check the complete hit envelope with POKEY read
hooks returning different paddle values; the emitted divider sequence must be
independent of those values. Also check pause/resume at every timer phase.
No correction was applied.

### M5-02 — loop re-entry wraps a negative distance and commands a ram

**Location:** `src/c/lifecycle.c:1204-1237`; contact consequence in
`src/hybrid/light-kernel.s:360-390`; authored two-loop swarm in
`assets/levels/level-01.json`.

During a loop, slot 2 holds at Y=64 (`32 + 2*16`). On re-entry into its dive,
the standoff code calculates an unsigned byte:

```c
light_scratch = (uint8_t)((uint8_t)(PLAYER_Y - light_work) - light_target_x);
```

Here `light_target_x` is temporarily the slot's vertical offset, 32. With the
player at legal Y=80 and the Interceptor at Y=64, the intended distance is
`80 - 64 - 32 = -16`. It wraps to 240, passes the `>= 56` far-distance test,
and makes the enemy dive four lines per frame. Subsequent negative distances
keep taking the dive branch. The source comment claiming that the supported
slot count prevents wrapping does not hold after a top-hold loop.

**Evidence and reproduction.** Two separate linked-code checks were used:

1. I used the admission setup from `tests/interceptor-loops.test.mjs`: a real
   Interceptor admission with wave flags `2 << 6`, copying its arrays to three
   slots as that test does, and advancing `enemy_light_tick`. At the first
   return from slot 2's hold, its age went 250, 253, 0 while Y stayed 64. With
   the player at Y=80 the next tick moved to 68; with Y=96 it backed off to 62.
   This establishes that the loop produces the relevant state using the
   production state machine, rather than merely assuming a re-entry state.
2. I placed that hand-off state into `measure-population-harness.mjs`'s
   initialized production frame runner: slot 2 live/free, archetype offset 24,
   HP=3, X=128, Y=64, age=0, one loop remaining, and a stationary player at
   X=124/Y=80 with 10 health. Four full gameplay-frame calls produced enemy Y
   **68, 72, 76, 80** and player health **10, 10, 10, 0**. The final contact
   entered the player's dying state and reduced the Interceptor's HP to 2.

These are controlled CPU-model fixtures with normal collision execution,
not measurements of PAL elapsed time or a naturally played whole-level run.
The source establishes why they are relevant: level 1 authors three looping
Interceptors, its slot ceiling admits slot 2, the top-hold code sets this Y,
and normal contact applies ten hull units. The player need not move into the
enemy in the demonstrated final approach. Decision AK's intentional upward
exit is not being questioned.

**Why the existing process missed it.** Loop tests park the player at Y=190;
most standoff checks concern slot 0 or a low player. The Light behavior model
also duplicates unsigned wrap arithmetic. Those checks prove agreement on
sampled states without checking the geometry where top-hold rows and slot
spacing cross the player's position.

**Recommended correction and regression:** make the distance comparison handle
negative separation explicitly within the existing movement policy. Exercise
all admitted slots through complete loops at upper/middle/lower player Y,
including a player who moves upward during the hold. Assert the no-ram
standoff behavior using geometric separation, independently of the production
byte arithmetic. Keep the existing catchability and lifetime checks.

### M5-03 — a stale hit timer owns a silent channel throughout boss loading

**Location:** `src/main.s:13221-13236` and `:2988-2999`;
`src/hybrid/gameplay-music.s:111-137`;
`src/hybrid/sector-reader.s:884-904`;
`src/c/lifecycle.c:731-757`, `:1443-1461`.

Boss entry stops the old music and calls `pause_silence_audio`. That clears
POKEY output, but retains `hit_timer`. The boss theme starts and the sector
reader continues to tick it while loading. Its publication routine refuses to
write channel 2 whenever `hit_timer != 0`, because the hit effect owns that
channel. However, `update_sound`, which advances that timer, does not run during
the load. The retained owner is silent and cannot expire. The banner therefore
plays bass without its lead until gameplay resumes.

This is reachable after a surviving hit by the last hostile projectile. The
projectile is consumed; the next frame's boss-entry drain can pass even though
13 hit-effect frames remain. A clear playfield does not imply an idle sound
effect. The music/SFX arbitration is valid within gameplay; the transition
fails to relinquish that ownership.

**Evidence and reproduction.** First, a `runBossEntry` control with a zero timer
was compared with an otherwise identical entry prepared with a live timer.
Then I verified the trigger through the production frame order rather than
relying on the injected timer:

- Start `measure-population-harness.boot({difficulty:1})`; position the
  Director at the shipped level's boss sector with its boss-due flag and the
  fighter sector state, no live Heavy/Light/debris, and a surviving player at
  X=124/Y=190 with 10 health.
- Place one ordinary hostile PULSE in slot 5 at X=130/Y=188, lifetime 100.
  Execute one production frame. The hit consumes the shot, reduces health to
  **9**, and leaves `hit_timer = 13`.
- Continue the next active-frame path with `boss-harness.cpuOver` and `Drive`
  on that same memory, watching `asm_boss_enter`, music ticks and POKEY writes,
  until the loaded boss returns to the main loop.

Boss entry was taken, the load completed, and the timer was still **13**.
The Drive fixture supplied **80 sector reads and 465 music ticks**. There were
**465 nonzero AUDC1 writes and zero nonzero AUDC2 writes** during the observed
entry. These tick/read counts describe this synthetic Drive transaction and
are **not** the native banner's host-frame count or load duration. The causal
proof is the production call order, timer ownership and completed entry;
the probe illustrates its consequence.

**Why the existing process missed it.** Entry tests begin with clean sound
state. Music preemption tests advance `update_sound` in their gameplay loop.
Hardware-audio tests validate SKCTL and real-SIO hand-back, not a nonzero hit
timer across entry. Consequently both halves can pass in isolation while the
combined transition loses a voice.

**Recommended correction and regression:** define which SFX survive this
transition and relinquish stale channel ownership when silencing them. Add
an end-to-end entry case with a surviving last-projectile hit, music enabled,
and a nonzero timer; require the banner's two voices to publish normally.
The same ownership check should cover the summary/menu boundaries. No fix was
applied, and no physical listening result is asserted.

### M5-04 — diagnostic-emulator cache can certify the wrong executable

**Location:** `scripts/sio-boot-repro.mjs:65-101`, especially the recursive copy
at line 74, `make` at line 99 and unconditional stamp at line 100.

Preparation copies the already-built wall-trace emulator, replaces its header
with the SIO-diagnostic header, patches `sio.c`, invokes `make`, and writes a
header hash as a cache stamp. On this macOS host the copied objects and the
new header/source received timestamps in the same second. GNU make 3.81 did
not rebuild them. The function then stamped the unchanged wall-trace executable
as a valid SIO-diagnostic build. Later calls trust the stamp and keep it.

**Observed failure, not a planted mutation.** After preparing the current
wall-trace emulator in the clone, I ran:

```sh
node --test --test-concurrency=1 tests/hardware-boot.test.mjs tests/hardware-audio.test.mjs
```

Five tests passed and both native tests failed. Their first underlying error
was **`voidstrike65 trace: missing DFTRACE_FRAMES`**: the diagnostic launcher
sets `DFSIO_*`, but it was running the wall-trace instrumentation.
Before repair of generated build products, both binaries had SHA-256
`b2bed0d2753ec2813db3746e8f4f44d9911dd1bb121204932e9eff8a6eff6645`.
The CPU object dependency file did name `voidstrike65_trace.h`; a missing
header dependency was not the cause. The observed nanosecond mtimes were:

| File in `build/atari800-sio-diag/src/` | `mtimeNs` |
| --- | ---: |
| `atari800-cpu.o` | 1792388714362702673 |
| `atari800-sio.o` | 1792388714453817954 |
| `voidstrike65_trace.h` | 1792388714495024847 |
| `sio.c` | 1792388714495362680 |
| `atari800` | 1792388714442286169 |

All share second 1792388714. `make -n -C build/atari800-sio-diag` reported
nothing to do. Running **`make clean` and `make -j8` only in this generated
emulator directory**, then rerunning the same two focused test files, produced
**7 passes, no failures, no skips**. No repository source was changed.

**Impact:** a fresh audit/test environment can fail its real-SIO diagnostics
and remain stuck behind a misleading freshness stamp. This is not evidence
of a game boot/audio failure. Nor does this observation establish a false
PASS in the current tests: they failed visibly. It weakens the reliability
of the validation tool that is supposed to distinguish hardware failures.

**Recommended correction and regression:** ensure the diagnostic copy cannot
reuse the wall-trace objects after changing instrumentation, and bind the
cache to the resulting executable as well as the header. The separate wall-trace
freshness check does not validate this secondary diagnostic copy. Test preparation
from an already-built source tree with equal-second timestamps, followed by
a second cached invocation. Do not classify this error as a known game test
failure or weaken the native assertions.

## Design concerns

No new owner design decision is requested by this audit. The three game
findings concern behavior contrary to existing contracts, and M5-04 concerns
reproducible validation. ATR-only distribution, PAL-only targeting, the
16-entry level capacity, shipped levels 1-2, debug-only access to boss regions
2-4, the accepted boss order and the documented hardware-smoke deferral are
not defects in this report.

The demonstrated process risks are concrete: a local fix for one write-only
register was not applied as a hardware invariant to its neighbor; movement
models shared production arithmetic without an independent separation check;
and subsystem tests started from clean states that omitted a legal transition.
These support focused cross-boundary regressions, not simply more nominal
replays or more assertions of compiled constants.
